# Presentation

## Is Enough Being Done? The Issue of High Crime in Some Toronto Neighbourhoods

This folder contains the presentation developed for the Toronto Open Data project.

The presentation transforms the data analysis into a story designed for a **non-technical audience**.

---

## Presentation Objective

The presentation addresses the question:

> **Is enough being done to address reported crime in Toronto neighbourhoods with elevated crime levels?**

The presentation uses data and visual evidence to explain the issue and support the final recommendation.

---

## Presentation Focus

The presentation examines:

* Which neighbourhoods have higher reported crime
* How crime levels compare between neighbourhoods
* Crime in relation to population
* Crime rates
* Changes in crime over time
* Types of reported crime
* Moss Park and the George Brown St. James Campus area
* Existing community safety efforts
* Recommendations based on the analysis

---

## Presentation Story

The presentation follows a data-storytelling structure:

1. **The Issue**
   Why neighbourhood crime is an important issue.

2. **The Objective**
   The question the analysis is designed to answer.

3. **The Data**
   Where the information comes from.

4. **Neighbourhood Comparison**
   Which selected neighbourhoods have higher reported crime.

5. **Population Context**
   Why crime counts need to be considered alongside population.

6. **Crime Rates**
   How population-adjusted measures change the comparison.

7. **Crime Trends**
   How reported crime changes over time.

8. **Crime Types**
   What categories contribute to reported crime.

9. **Moss Park Context**
   The relevance of Moss Park to the George Brown St. James Campus area.

10. **Existing Safety Measures**
    What actions or initiatives are already documented.

11. **Recommendation**
    What the evidence suggests should be considered.

12. **Conclusion**
    The main takeaway from the analysis.

---

## Visuals

The presentation uses charts, tables, and other visuals to communicate the findings.

Examples include:

* Crime by neighbourhood
* Crime rate comparisons
* Population comparisons
* Crime trends over time
* Crime by category
* Neighbourhood/location context

Visuals are used to make the findings understandable without requiring the audience to understand the underlying Excel or Access processes.

---

## Audience

The intended audience is non-technical.

The presentation therefore focuses on:

**What does the data show?**

**Why does it matter?**

**What can we learn from it?**

**What should be considered next?**

Rather than:

**How was the Excel formula created?**

**How was the database query written?**

**How does the software work?**

---

## Presentation Principle

The project follows the principle:

> **Do not stop at finding a pattern. Ask, "So what?"**

Each major chart or finding should contribute to the overall story and support the final recommendation.
