# Documentation

## Project Overview

This project examines reported crime across selected Toronto neighbourhoods using publicly available data from the City of Toronto.

The project was developed as a Business Analytics project to demonstrate how public data can be used to investigate a community issue, identify meaningful patterns, and support a data-informed recommendation.

## Research Question

**Is enough being done to address reported crime in Toronto neighbourhoods with elevated crime levels?**

The project focuses on understanding differences in reported crime between neighbourhoods while considering population, crime rates, crime types, changes over time, and community context.

## Issue Background

Crime can affect how residents, students, employees, and visitors experience a neighbourhood. However, simply identifying which neighbourhood has the highest number of reported incidents does not provide a complete picture.

A neighbourhood with a larger population or more commercial and transportation activity may naturally have more reported incidents. Therefore, the project considers multiple measures rather than relying only on total crime counts.

## Neighbourhoods Examined

The analysis focuses on the following Toronto neighbourhoods:

* Waterfront Communities-The Island
* West Humber-Clairville
* Bay Street Corridor
* Church-Yonge Corridor
* Moss Park

Moss Park receives additional attention because of its proximity to George Brown Polytechnic's St. James Campus and its relevance to the project context.

## Research Objectives

The project aims to:

1. Identify neighbourhoods with higher levels of reported crime.
2. Compare reported crime across the selected neighbourhoods.
3. Examine population differences between neighbourhoods.
4. Calculate and compare crime rates where appropriate.
5. Examine changes in reported crime over time.
6. Identify the types of crime contributing to reported totals.
7. Consider community and neighbourhood context when interpreting the results.
8. Review existing safety-related efforts and initiatives relevant to the issue.
9. Develop a data-informed recommendation based on the findings.

## Methodology

The project follows a data-to-decision approach:

**Collect → Clean → Compare → Analyze → Visualize → Interpret → Recommend**

### 1. Collect

Relevant public datasets were identified using the City of Toronto Open Data Portal.

### 2. Clean

The data was reviewed and prepared for analysis. This includes checking neighbourhood names, selecting relevant variables, organizing the data, and preparing information for comparison.

### 3. Compare

The selected neighbourhoods were compared using measures such as reported crime counts, population, crime rates, crime categories, and historical trends.

### 4. Analyze

The analysis looks for meaningful differences and patterns rather than relying on a single measure.

### 5. Visualize

Charts, tables, and other visuals are used to communicate findings to a non-technical audience.

### 6. Interpret

The results are considered within the context of population, neighbourhood characteristics, crime type, time period, and location.

### 7. Recommend

The findings are used to develop a data-informed recommendation addressing the project question.

## Key Considerations

Reported crime data should be interpreted carefully.

A higher number of reported incidents does not automatically mean that every person in a neighbourhood experiences the same level of risk. Crime counts can be influenced by factors such as:

* Population size
* Commercial activity
* Daytime population
* Public transportation
* Tourism and visitors
* Reporting behaviour
* Policing and enforcement
* Neighbourhood geography
* Type of crime

Similarly, a lower number of reported incidents does not necessarily mean that a neighbourhood has no safety concerns.

## Project Decision

The project does not treat total crime count as the only measure of neighbourhood safety.

Instead, the decision-making approach considers multiple indicators together, including reported crime, population, crime rates, crime types, historical trends, and neighbourhood context.

The resulting recommendation is intended to support a **data-informed, neighbourhood-specific approach to community safety**, rather than applying the same response to every neighbourhood.

Potential areas for consideration include community-based safety programs, improvements to public spaces, community outreach, support services, and continued monitoring of crime patterns.

## Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the project:

* The data represents reported or recorded incidents and may not represent all crime that occurs.
* Neighbourhoods differ in population, land use, commercial activity, transportation, and other characteristics.
* Crime counts and crime rates measure different aspects of the issue.
* Changes in reported crime may be affected by multiple factors.
* The analysis cannot establish that a particular factor directly caused changes in crime.

## Intended Audience

The final presentation is designed for a **non-technical audience**.

The project focuses on communicating:

* What the data shows
* Why the issue matters
* How neighbourhoods compare
* What can be learned from the data
* What factors should be considered
* What action or approach the findings support

The goal is to communicate the **"So what?"** behind the data rather than focusing on the technical process used to produce the analysis.
